Model assumptions: every trade risks the same fixed amount, wins pay the same R multiple, and trades are independent coin flips at your win rate. Real trading has streaks of correlated losses, slippage beyond the modeled fees, and days you break your own rules — so treat the blowup rate here as an optimistic floor, not a ceiling.

Card counter formulas (per trade, win W = R·risk − fees, loss L = risk + fees): EV = p·W − q·L.  SD = √(p·W² + q·L² − EV²).  N₀ = (SD/EV)² — the number of trades where cumulative EV first equals one standard deviation, the counter's definition of "the long run". Risk of ruin = e−2·EV·B/SD² with bankroll B = the max drawdown; it assumes a fixed bankroll, so a trailing drawdown makes the true number worse (compare it with the simulated blow rate). Kelly fraction = p − q/(W/L), applied to the drawdown as your bankroll — half Kelly is the professional's usual compromise between growth and variance.

Trailing drawdown trails the balance high-water mark and locks once it reaches the starting balance (Topstep-style). "End of day" updates the trail from the end-of-day balance; "every trade" updates it in real time (Apex/MFF-style, which is harsher). Educational tool — not financial advice.
